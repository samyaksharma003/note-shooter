Game Modes
Classic›
Notes fall from the top. Play the correct note before it hits the bottom. You have 3 lives — miss 3 and it's game over. Score and speed increase as you hit more notes. Extra lives are earned at streaks of 50, 150 and 300.
60s Timer›
Same as Classic but with a 60-second countdown instead of lives. Score as many points as you can before time runs out. Great for quick sessions and leaderboard runs. Completing it unlocks the Speed Run achievement.
Scale›
Pick a root note and a scale type — only notes from that scale will fall. This trains you to internalize which notes belong together. Your fingers and ears will start recognizing scale patterns naturally through repetition.
Practice›
No falling notes, no pressure. A note name appears on screen — find it on your fretboard and play it. The fretboard below lights up all matching positions when you play correctly. The best way to build your fretboard map from scratch.
Difficulty Levels
Beginner›
Slow notes, long gaps, max 2 on screen. Only natural notes (C D E F G A B) — no sharps. Ideal when you are just starting out and building basic note recognition.
Intermediate›
Moderate speed, shorter gaps, up to 3 notes on screen. A good challenge once you have natural notes memorized and want to push your reaction time.
Pro›
Fast speed, up to 4 notes simultaneously. Demands quick recognition and immediate execution. Recommended once you can reliably clear Intermediate without losing lives.
Shredder›
Maximum speed, up to 5 notes at once, very short intervals. Designed for players who already have solid fretboard knowledge. Enable Sharp Notes in Settings to push it even further.
Tips
How does note detection work?›
Your microphone listens for the pitch you play using autocorrelation. Play cleanly and let each note ring briefly — muted or buzzing notes may not register. Keep your mic close to your guitar and make sure background noise is low.
Sharp Notes and Octave Mode?›
Sharp Notes (in Settings) adds C# D# F# G# A# to the note pool, making the game harder and more complete. Octave Mode labels each note with its octave number (e.g. C3 or E4), helping you pinpoint the exact fretboard position rather than just the note name.
